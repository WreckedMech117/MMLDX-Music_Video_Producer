Does the 8-step bundle hold up?

Three shots from Harder Faster — Third Video, each rendered twice at the same seed: once on default (20 steps, res_multistep, no LoRA) and once on turbo-references2v (8 steps, euler, minimax_h3_turbo_v4 @ 1.0). Same prompt, same references, same song window.

Read this first.
  • A bundle change re-rolls the take. Same seed, same prompt, but a different performance — not the same one rendered better or worse. Nothing here is a frame-by-frame diff, and comparing frame N to frame N would be meaningless.
  • Stills for the two singing shots are sampled on named consonants, at the song second the word begins, because that is where mouth shape is legible. Each column states its song second. The last column of each singing shot is an open-vowel control: the mouth should be open there, not closed.
  • Audio numbers are comparative, not absolute. H3 regenerates its own audio rather than copying the song, so a correlation well below 1 is correct behaviour. Lag aliases on periodic material — read a difference between arms as signal, an absolute value as a hint. A silent track is reported as absent, never scored.

The analyst's read

Evidence, not a recommendation. The ruling is the Director's and belongs to the pictures above.

Lip-sync — the two singing shots

Neither bundle is systematically better, and neither is precise. Across the six consonant samples the result is mixed: at 67.16 s (/m/ of “my”) default has the lips together and turbo has the jaw dropped — default is right there. At 58.94 s (/w/ of “wolf”) turbo shows clear lip rounding and default barely parts the lips — turbo is right there. At 68.26 s (/m/ of “make”) both close the lips; at 60.24 s (/m/ of “moon”) neither does. That is approximate bilabial timing in both arms, not a turbo regression.

The audio agrees and is the stronger evidence, because it is measured rather than eyeballed: envelope correlation differs by 0.005 or less on every shot (0.8454 / 0.8404 close-up, 0.9876 / 0.9825 wide, 0.9664 / 0.9638 b-roll) and the lag is identical to 0.1 ms in all three pairs. Whatever H3 is doing to lock its regenerated vocal to the song, 8 steps does it as well as 20.

Picture

Turbo is not softer — it is sharper and punchier. Measured high-frequency energy is higher on every shot (+127%, +152%, +52%), encoded bytes per frame are higher on both singing shots, and the look is more contrast, more saturation, stronger edges. The cost is texture realism: at high zoom turbo's skin reads waxier and its mid-tone micro-texture is flatter, the familiar few-step-LoRA trade of acutance for grain. Eyes and irises are coherent in both. No hands are clearly in frame in any of the six takes, so hands are untested.

Turbo's frame-to-frame change is higher on the two singing shots (+87%, +38%) and lower on the b-roll (−23%). Read that carefully: turbo also held a tighter, more active framing on both singing shots, so some of that is real motion rather than instability. The b-roll — where both arms hold the same slow drift — is the fair comparison, and there turbo is the steadier of the two.

What both bundles do wrong, equally

The two singing shots do not stay on their subject. Both arms hold the requested framing for roughly the first half, then pull back, let the character walk out, and spend the remainder on the empty warehouse — despite the submitted prompt asking for “one continuous take”. Scene-change detection finds zero hard cuts in all six takes, so this is a continuous camera drift, not a cut. It is an H3-and-prompt problem and it is present in the 20-step baseline too; switching bundles neither causes nor fixes it. At the same seed the two arms converge on strikingly similar compositions — compare the 70.60 s pair, which is the same empty warehouse twice.

The measured saving is smaller than 4.62×.
  • Per step the two bundles cost the same: 20.6 s (default, b-roll) against 22.8 s (turbo, b-roll) — turbo is marginally dearer per step, for the LoRA. The saving is therefore the step count, 20 → 8, and nothing else.
  • On the one pair where both arms ran clean and back to back — the b-roll, 158 frames — it is 479.1 s vs 236.6 s = 2.0× on ComfyUI exec, or 2.26× on sampling alone. Fixed overhead (model load, conditioning, VAE decode; ~65–75 s a render) dilutes the 2.5× step ratio to about 2×.
  • The turbo half of the original estimate checks out exactly: the archived synthetic probe for 158 frames read 236.8 s and this run measured 236.6 s. It is the default half that looks overstated — 4.62× would put a 158-frame default render near 1094 s, and it measured 479.1 s.
  • wide/default's 2929.5 s is not a bundle number. It ran during a VRAM pressure episode (148–185 s/step against 17–22 s/step everywhere else, power down to ~160 W at 99% utilisation, 631 MiB free). Its sampler clock was also lost when the harness process was killed. Excluded from every ratio above.
