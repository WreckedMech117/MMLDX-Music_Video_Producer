Does “soft skin” change the waxiness?

One shot — Close-up, singing, 141 frames, seed 14, song window 66.58–71.69 s with a 0.25 s lead — rendered on turbo-references2v (8 steps, euler, minimax_h3_turbo_v4 @ 1.0) three times in one warm session. Arm A is the shot's prose verbatim. Arm B is that prose with two words appended and nothing else changed. The third arm is the control prose at a different seed, and it is there to say how far this measurement moves when nothing changes.

Read this first.
  • A prompt change re-rolls the take. Same seed, same bundle, different prose gives a different performance — not the same performance rendered differently. There is no frame-by-frame before/after here. Stills are cut at matched song seconds; the acting underneath them differs.
  • n is 1 per arm. A single pair can show a large effect or a flat null. It cannot size a small one.
  • Nothing was added to the application. The clause exists only inside this experiment's own submissions. No prompt, project, adapter or default was written, and this page is not a recommendation to add it anywhere.
  • The lyric under this window is copyrighted and is not reproduced. Sung words are named by their first letter only.

The answer: we cannot tell from this.

The clause moves the skin measure -11.1% — in the direction that would mean it works. But re-rolling the take with the unchanged prose moves the same number 15.3%. The effect is a third of the noise, and a prompt change is a re-roll.

Two things argue further against reading the movement as texture. The contrast-normalised version of the same measure goes the other way (+6.2%): what little changed is a slightly flatter face overall, not micro-texture coming back. And the gap the clause would have to close is −55.9% — the distance from the 8-step bundle to the 20-step one on this same instrument.

By eye, arm B's skin is not less waxy. The sheen on the forehead, the nose and the cheekbone is present in both arms at every matched moment. Judge that yourself from the stills below — the number is not the verdict, and neither is this paragraph.

What would settle it is three seeds per arm, about half an hour of GPU. That is not run here.

The measure

detail is mean Laplacian energy — high-frequency content, the same instrument the bundle comparison used, so these land on that table's scale. skin detail is that same statistic over the face rectangle only and over the first 2.58 s, which is the span where the face fills the frame in every arm; after that each take makes its own camera move to its own destination and the whole-take figure starts comparing content instead of texture. skin detail, normalised divides out each take's own contrast, which separates “more grain” from “flatter picture”.

Two floors, and the re-roll one is the one that applies. The same-payload floor is what a byte-identical graph at a byte-identical seed produces in a different session — small, 2.1% on detail, which is about the 2.5% these renders reproduce to. The re-roll floor is what a different take of the same prose produces, and a prompt change is a re-roll, so that is the bar. A number has to clear the re-roll floor before it means anything about the clause.

One thing worth knowing that this run turned up. The archived arm A and this run's arm A are the same graph at the same seed, and their pixels are not the same picture: mean absolute difference 20.3/255, pixel correlation 0.48. That is less alike than arm A is to arm B (18.7/255, correlation 0.55). The summary statistics reproduce to a few percent; the frames do not reproduce at all. Anything that plans to reuse an old take as a control should know that.

The timings are recorded, not compared. Arm B began with 8976 MiB resident where arm A began with 27170 MiB — something evicted the model stack between the two arms without being asked to — so arm B paid a reload that arm A did not. That is why the two differ by more than the 2.5% warm renders reproduce to, and it is not a property of the clause.
